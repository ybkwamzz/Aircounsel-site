RESEARCH SYNTHESIS : REF AC-ARCH-2026-11-20 · TECHNICAL ARCHITECTURE & REPOSITORY AUDIT

Global Legal-Data Infrastructure, Ingestion Contracts, and the Case for Provenance-Preserving Law

PUBLISHED: 20 NOVEMBER 2026  |  AUTHOR: KWAMEH J. DANDE  |  SCOPE: AKN 3.1 · FRBR · ELI/ECLI · LEGALRULEML · INGESTION AUDIT

The central question in legal-information engineering is not simply how to collect and index the world's laws into a single search table. It is how to design interoperable infrastructure that preserves the identity, authority, provenance, jurisdiction, licensing, and temporal validity of every legal instrument throughout its lifecycle, and how to prove that downstream systems have retrieved and represented it faithfully.

A search engine can locate a document. A flat database can store it. A language model can summarise its prose. Yet none of those operations, by itself, establishes that the retrieved text is authoritative, complete, applicable to a specific cohort, or legally in force on the date that matters.

Part I : The Eight-Layer Legal-Data Infrastructure Stack

A conventional scraping platform often normalises legislation, regulations, and court judgments into a flat seven-field schema (_id, _source, _type, title, text, date, url). While useful for basic keyword discovery, flat normalisation discards the properties that govern legal meaning. Trustworthy infrastructure separates eight distinct engineering responsibilities:

LayerEngineering ResponsibilityFailure Mode Classification
1. Source AuthorityWho issued the instrument? Is it an official gazette, an authorised reproduction, or a secondary mirror?Data Failure: Collecting from an unauthoritative or superseded mirror.
2. AcquisitionWas the complete resource retrieved, including pagination, schedules, annexes, and attachments?Data Failure: Truncated pagination or missing schedules.
3. Ingestion IntegrityWere incomplete crawls, WAF interstitials (including HTTP 200 challenges), and broken pages quarantined?Data Failure: Partial crawls advancing freshness checkpoints.
4. Structural ValidationDoes the parsed output conform to its formal schema before reaching persistent storage?Data Failure: Logging validation warnings while still writing invalid records.
5. Identity & ProvenanceCan the legal work, expression, manifestation, byte digest, and transformation lineage be verified?Data Failure: Treating a mutable URL or OCR text as an immutable primary source.
6. Temporal & Jurisdictional ModellingWhich provision version applies, when, across which territorial extent, and to which transitional cohort?Legal-Context Failure: Applying current consolidated text to a historical or transitional event.
7. Relationships & IndexingHow do amending Acts, commencement orders, unapplied effects, and judicial citations alter legal state?Legal-Context Failure: Missing an unapplied amendment or subsequent overruling judgment.
8. Retrieval & Answer FidelityDid the downstream system retrieve the exact passage and represent its legal holding faithfully?Reasoning Failure: Real source citation paired with an invented quote or misstated holding.

Part II : Empirical Case Study : Shared Scraper Frameworks (Legal Data Hunter)

To examine how these eight layers behave in practice, we inspected the open-source collection repository of Legal Data Hunter (worldwidelaw/legal-sources), which coordinates 960+ collection scripts across 110+ countries on top of a shared Python abstraction (common/base_scraper.py, common/validators.py, and common/storage.py).

Using a shared base class across hundreds of jurisdiction-specific adapters is a sound architectural pattern for standardising rate limiting, status tracking (status.yaml), and deduplicated JSONL storage. However, it also concentrates structural risk: any weakness in the base ingestion contract propagates across the entire collection fleet.

Furthermore, distinguishing between mutable legislation (upsert with version tracking) and append-only case law is a necessary start, but insufficient as a complete legal model: earlier legislative versions must remain historically queryable by point-in-time date, and while a published court judgment's text is immutable, its precedential authority mutates whenever a subsequent decision distinguishes, follows, or overrules it. Document immutability does not imply legal-relationship immutability.

Part III : Source-Code Inspection Findings & Reclassification

An inspection of common/base_scraper.py, common/validators.py, common/storage.py, and individual source adapters establishes three primary ingestion-contract findings and several important source-level nuances:

Finding 1 : Non-Atomic Checkpoint Advancement on Partial Bootstrap Runs

In common/base_scraper.py, the bootstrap() execution loop catches exceptions occurring during collection and records them in run statistics and the status object. However, the completion block subsequently flushes storage, updates last_bootstrap and last_run, appends to run_history, and saves status.yaml without making checkpoint advancement conditional on zero collection errors or verified source completeness. Any downstream scheduler or consumer that treats last_run as a verified freshness watermark cannot distinguish a complete crawl from a partial crawl that encountered mid-stream exceptions.

Finding 2 : Schema Validation Operates as a Warning Log Rather Than a Write Gate

In bootstrap(), records are passed to SchemaValidator.validate(record):

is_valid, errors = self.validator.validate(record)
if not is_valid:
    logger.warning(f"Validation errors for {record.get('_id', '?')}: {errors}")

After logging the warning, execution continues directly into storage persistence, allowing structurally invalid records to be written. Across the three primary execution paths, validation enforcement is asymmetric:

Execution PathObserved Validation Behaviour
bootstrap()Calls self.validator.validate(record), logs a warning on failure, and continues writing the record to storage.
update()No corresponding SchemaValidator call on incremental updates.
bootstrap_fast()No corresponding SchemaValidator call on concurrent batch runs.

Having a validator class in the repository is not equivalent to enforcing a validated write gate.

Finding 3 : Coverage-Gap Telemetry Does Not Gate Completion State

The codebase has evolved to include explicit coverage-gap telemetry (record_coverage_gap(), clear_coverage_gap(), and _report_coverage_gaps()), persisting gap records into status.yaml. While this is a meaningful operational improvement over silent page loss, the presence of unresolved coverage_gaps still does not block the completion logic from advancing last_run.

Finding 4 : Multi-Jurisdictional HTTP 200 WAF Challenges & Active Remediations

Government legal portals frequently fail in ways that defeat transport-level HTTP status checks. Two documented examples in the repository illustrate why HTTP 200 OK never guarantees valid legal content:

  • Indonesia : Bank Indonesia (sources/ID/BI/bootstrap.py, Issue #1470): The former jdih.bi.go.id endpoint returned an F5/BIG-IP rejection page with an HTTP 200 status code to requests outside Indonesia, causing earlier scraper runs to interpret the interstitial as an empty page and report a 0-record crawl. The current ID/BI adapter remediates this by inspecting the response body for explicit rejection markers and raising an exception.
  • Poland : SAOS vs. Common Courts Portal (sources/PL/SAOS/bootstrap.py & PL/CommonCourts Issue #1838): The repository maintains separate Polish judicial adapters. PL/SAOS queries the SAOS public API with pagination, incremental modification-date filtering, and explicit exceptions on repeated page failure. Separately, PL/CommonCourts (Portal Orzeczen Sadow Powszechnych, orzeczenia.ms.gov.pl, logged under Issue #1838 on 12 October 2026) is blocked because every path returns an F5 Shape/BIG-IP ASM JavaScript challenge as HTTP 200 (window["bobcmn"], /TSPD/ cookies).
  • Additional Active Codebase Improvements: Recent commits also introduced _coerce_specs() in common/validators.py to support bare-string field definitions, fixed silent PDF exception swallowing (e.g. US/FINRA-Awards Issue #1827), and added classify_fetch_updates() to distinguish genuine incremental implementations from stubs.

Part IV : The Global Legal-Data Ecosystem & Standards Stack

Six complementary reference initiatives and five formal standards illustrate how different layers of the global legal-data problem have been tackled:

1. Legislation.gov.uk (UK National Archives)

The premier reference for point-in-time statutory publishing. Uses an FRBR-derived model separating the Legal Work, Enacted Expression, Revised Point-in-Time Expression (by date and geographical extent), and Manifestation (CLML, AKN, HTML, PDF). Exposes unapplied effects and a paginated Publication Log (20 entries per page), while candidly documenting historical pre-1988/1994 coverage boundaries.

2. OASIS Akoma Ntoso 3.1 (Standard, Oct 2026)

Announced as an OASIS Standard on 13 October 2026 (Akoma Ntoso Version 2.0, Part 2: Specifications, defining AKN 3.1). Expands structured XML containers for schedules, annexes, judicial decisions (witness, court, judgmentSummary), non-Gregorian calendars, and multilingual passages without erasing national drafting conventions.

3. EUR-Lex, ELI & ECLI

ELI provides persistent URIs, FRBR-informed metadata (RDFa/JSON-LD), and synchronisation feeds for legislation. ECLI (ECLI:Country:Court:Year:Number) standardises judicial decision citations across participating courts, demonstrating that identifier standards still depend on national publisher adoption.

4. WorldLII & AfricanLII

Demonstrate federated legal discovery across independent Legal Information Institutes (BAILII, AustLII, CanLII, SAFLII, Open Law Africa) without forcing nations to surrender custody to a single commercial database. AfricanLII explicitly prohibits unauthorised bulk scraping, provides managed APIs, and flags unverified OCR.

5. CourtListener & Harvard CAP

Free Law Project's CourtListener models opinions, dockets, judges, and citation graphs (cites, distinguishes, overrules) across 10M+ opinions. Harvard's Caselaw Access Project digitised 40M+ pages into 6.7M+ US cases spanning 360 years (through 2020), illustrating the distinction between a deep historical digitisation archive and a live incremental feed.

6. OASIS LegalRuleML (Standard, Aug 2021)

Approved as an OASIS Standard on 30 August 2021 (LegalRuleML Core Specification v1.0). Moves beyond document markup to model formal legal norms: subjects, preconditions, deontic obligations, permissions, prohibitions, temporal scope, and links back to source statutory provisions.

StandardPrimary Architectural ResponsibilityWhat It Cannot Establish Alone
CLMLUK-specific statutory markup, point-in-time revisions, and unapplied legislative effects.Cross-jurisdictional judicial or civil-law document structures.
Akoma Ntoso 3.1Interoperable XML structure for legislative, parliamentary, and judicial documents.Whether the source publisher was authoritative or the ingestion crawl was complete.
ELI / ECLIPersistent URI identification and machine-readable metadata for statutes and judgments.Whether non-participating courts or historic gazettes are completely indexed.
LegalRuleML 1.0Formal representation of legal rules, obligations, thresholds, and defeasible logic.How a court or regulated practitioner exercises discretionary legal judgement.

Part V : Four-Dimensional Time, Provenance, Licensing & Answer Fidelity

1. The Four Dimensions of Legal Time

Consider an Act where an amendment is enacted on 1 January 2025, commences on 1 April 2025, and is published in a revised consolidation on 15 April 2025. To answer what the law required on 20 February 2025, infrastructure must model four distinct temporal axes:

  • Dimension 1 : Legal Validity Time (Valid Time): The exact window during which a provision or transitional cohort rule legally applies.
  • Dimension 2 : Legal Event Time: When the underlying legislative or judicial act occurred (Royal Assent, Statement of Changes laid before Parliament, commencement order, repeal).
  • Dimension 3 : Publication & Consolidation Time (Availability Time): When the authority released the text or consolidated an amendment (tracking unapplied effects prior to consolidation).
  • Dimension 4 : System-Recorded Time (Transaction Time): When the platform ingested, validated, and cryptographically pinned the specification.

2. Provenance & The Computational Licensing Registry

A SHA-256 digest proves that a captured binary has not mutated since acquisition; it does not prove that the source was authoritative or that the operator holds legal rights to index or train AI models on it. Crucially, publicly readable legal data is not automatically unrestricted for commercial indexing or computational analysis.

For example, The National Archives' Open Justice Licence (v2.0) for Find Case Law permits reading and quoting judgments, but explicitly excludes computational analysis of the licensed material and compiling a search engine index; re-users must apply for a separate Transactional / Computational Analysis Licence. Similarly, AfricanLII restricts unauthorised bulk harvesting. Every ingested record therefore requires both a 10-field provenance envelope and an explicit licensing registry covering retrieval, bulk download, commercial use, computational analysis, search indexing, redistribution, and attribution.

3. Reference Integrity vs. Passage & Answer Fidelity

As highlighted in recent engineering discussions (including Dean Hoffman's critique of "Nothing invented" claims), returning a genuine URL to a real case eliminates fabricated case citations, but does not prevent a language model from inventing a quotation, citing a dissenting opinion as the holding, or applying an outdated provision. Trustworthy retrieval separates four verification checks:

Verification TierEngineering QuestionRequired Evidence Artifact
1. Reference IntegrityDoes the cited instrument or judgment exist at an authoritative source?Verified URI, publisher identity, ECLI/ELI, and source SHA-256 hash.
2. Passage IntegrityDoes the exact section or paragraph contain the quoted words?Passage-level evidence object with character offsets and exact verbatim span check.
3. Interpretation IntegrityDoes the summary accurately reflect the court's holding rather than a party's submission or dissent?Structural section tagging (e.g. AKN 3.1 judgmentBody vs. dissent) and human practitioner review.
4. Applicability IntegrityWas that provision in force for this jurisdiction, territorial extent, and statutory cohort date?4D temporal validity query and cohort applicability gate.

Part VI : Unified Reference Architecture & The 9-State Ingestion FSM

Rather than forcing common-law statutes, civil codes, EU directives, and African regional gazettes into a single rigid substantive schema, we propose a Federated Common Core with Jurisdiction-Specific Extensions (bridging UK CLML, EU ELI/ECLI/AKN 3.1, and local court identifiers into a typed Temporal Legal Knowledge Graph).

At the acquisition boundary, every resource must traverse a strict 9-State Ingestion Finite State Machine:

Ingestion StateContractual Requirement
DISCOVEREDResource URI and native identifier catalogued from feed, sitemap, or gazette index.
FETCHEDComplete transport response captured; verified free of HTTP 200 WAF/CAPTCHA interstitials.
PARSEDContent extracted; OCR length/encoding checks verified without silent exception swallowing.
STRUCTURALLY_VALIDATEDRecord passes mandatory schema validation gate (hard write-block on failure).
SEMANTICALLY_VALIDATEDJurisdiction, temporal metadata, and required provision identifiers verified.
RECONCILEDPagination totals and expected feed inventories matched against source catalogue.
COMMITTEDImmutable raw snapshot hash, transformed record, and checkpoint atomically persisted.
INCOMPLETERun experienced unresolved coverage gaps or partial page failures; checkpoint frozen.
QUARANTINEDFailed validation or licensing check; isolated from downstream search and AI retrieval.
Core Invariant: An INCOMPLETE or QUARANTINED state must never silently transition to COMMITTED or advance the freshness checkpoint watermark. Furthermore, retrieval must surface an explicit Unresolved Legal Change Exposure metric whenever an indexed provision has an unapplied amendment or pending commencement order.

Part VII : How This Connects to AirCounsel STRATA & UK Immigration Law

Global legal-data collection and regulated law-firm practice infrastructure are distinct systems, yet they rely on the exact same engineering physics: a downstream system must never silently convert upstream uncertainty or incomplete data into apparent legal certainty.

Rather than boiling the ocean, UK immigration law provides the ideal high-complexity proving ground for this architecture:

  • Overlapping Primary, Secondary & Sub-Statutory Rules: The Immigration Act 1971, UK Borders Act 2007, Statutory Instruments, and frequent HC Statements of Changes to the Immigration Rules.
  • Multi-Cohort Temporal Validity: Skilled Worker and Appendix FM applications require deterministic point-in-time evaluation across pre-4 April 2024 transitional cohorts, post-22 July 2025 salary thresholds, and 2026 rule updates (tracked live in aircounsel.uk/changes.html).
  • Kernel-Level AI Containment & Evidentiary Ledgers: Inside AirCounsel STRATA V5.0, ActorKind.LLM_AGENT is structurally barred at the database kernel from mutating case state or emitting visa eligibility opinions (preserving s.84 Immigration and Asylum Act 1999 and SRA Code 6.2 compliance), while every document and gate decision is byte-pinned into a 30-day causal attribution ledger.
